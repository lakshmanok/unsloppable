## Scene Description & Thematic Analysis

- **The Setting**: A minimalist bathroom drawn with Saul Steinberg's spare, conceptual architectural line economy and generous negative space.
- **The Core Premise (Surprisal & Commonsense Reasoning)**: The cartoon addresses the fundamental cognitive and probabilistic question: *“What would surprise you more, to see a flock of ducks swimming in a lake or quacking in a bathtub?”* In information theory and cognitive science, a high-probability event (ducks swimming in a lake) has near-zero surprisal, whereas an out-of-context, low-probability event (ducks quacking in a domestic bathtub) generates overwhelming surprisal.
- **The Satirical Inversion**: Rather than posing the dilemma as a dry academic exercise, the cartoon grounds it in bureaucratic and literalist absurdity. The Common Man experiences an acute domestic crisis and calls for emergency help. When the specialist arrives, he looks at both the domestic chaos in the bathtub and the natural scenery through the window with equal, literal indifference—failing to see why one flock requires an emergency call while the other does not.
- **The Contrast**: Through the open window, nature is calm, orderly, and expected. Inside the bathtub, reality is wet, loud, and disorderly. The Common Man stands trapped between the two, clutching his towel in speechless bewilderment.
